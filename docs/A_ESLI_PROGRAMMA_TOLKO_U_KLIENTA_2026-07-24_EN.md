# "What if the program is only on the client's side?" — the honest answer

The situation: I write an invoice for a million in Word/Excel and send it by
email. On the way the letter is intercepted and the figure is changed. The client
receives an invoice for a different amount. How will he tell that I wrote
something else, if the program is only on his side?

---

## The direct answer: no way — if all I did was the letter

If I simply created a file and sent it by email, **the client won't know.** His
program has no copy of my real invoice — it only has the one that arrived. The
substituted figure is the only thing he sees. There's nothing to compare against.

This is not a weakness of the program. It's a law: **you cannot notice a
substitution if you have nothing to compare against.** You need an untouched
original — and there isn't one.

---

## Why a program on the client's side alone is not enough

To catch a substitution you need **two things by two different roads:**

1. the document itself (I send this by email);
2. an independent **mark** from me — a fingerprint of the real figure.

The interceptor rules the **mail road**. If the mark rides in the same letter — he
changes both the invoice and the mark at once. It's useless.

So the mark must travel by **a different road**, one the interceptor can't reach.

---

## What I, the sender, must do (one action)

At the moment I make the invoice — **"seal" it.** One button. The program takes a
fingerprint of the real figure and places it in a **shared registry "under
glass"** — not in the letter, but in a separate place that both sides can see and
that the interceptor does not control.

> I leave a fingerprint at the source. The interceptor touches the letter —
> but does not touch the registry.

---

## What the client now sees

The client receives the letter and reconciles it **not against my copy, but
against the fingerprint in the registry.**

- the figures match → this is my invoice, untouched;
- the figure differs → the program shows: **it was 1,000,000, it became
  9,000,000, the substitution is right here.**

He learns that I wrote something else — because my fingerprint lies where the
interceptor couldn't get to it.

---

## Why the interceptor won't substitute the mark too

- the mark is **not inside the letter** — it's in the registry, by a different
  road;
- the registry is **append-only**, whoever wrote first stays;
- I wrote the fingerprint **at the moment the invoice was born** — earlier than
  the interceptor;
- his late forgery won't rewrite my early record — it only "draws itself on the
  glass" and gets exposed.

---

## The main takeaway

**The program cannot be only on the client's side.** It's two touches:

- **I** place the mark at the moment the invoice is created (the sender);
- **the client** reconciles on receipt.

The mark on my side is one button, or fully automatic inside the program where I
write invoices (this is exactly the "door" the buyer embeds). Without a mark at
the source there is nothing to catch with — not because the product is weak, but
because there's nothing to compare against.

---

_In one line: for the client to catch a substitution, when sending I must leave a
fingerprint of the real figure by a road the interceptor can't reach. Then the
client reconciles what arrived against it — and the substitution is visible._
