## 2. Mechanism (author's testimony)

First step: **each word is enclosed in brackets** — `(word)`.
Visually clear: this is a meaningful unit, the bracket bounds it on both
sides. This is the first step toward understanding and beginning decipherment.

A fresh layer (thought up by the author 2026-07-22):
**a hierarchy of nesting with different brackets.**
```
( )  — encloses a WORD
[ ]  — encloses a SENTENCE

[ (word) (word) (word) ]  = a sentence of words
```
Different bracket types = different levels of meaning (word ⊂ sentence).
The future reader sees not only the word boundaries but also the grouping into
larger units — a self-describing grammar of containers.

## 3. What the mechanism gives a decipherer (LLM framing, verifiable)

1. **Segmentation** — where a unit of meaning ends. The first hard
   problem of unreadable scripts (Linear A, the Indus script).
2. **"Content is here"** — separates the sign from damage/ornament.
3. **Reading direction** — `(` ≠ `)`; the asymmetry itself encodes which
   way to read (a symmetric mark `|word|` does not give this).
4. **Hierarchy of meaning** — the nesting `[( )]` gives levels without knowledge of
   the language: a small container inside a large one = a word inside a sentence.

## 4. Historical precedent (LLM framing, fact)

The intuition independently rediscovered the mechanism that actually cracked
Egyptian hieroglyphs: **the cartouche** — the oval frame around royal names.
Champollion (1822) entered decipherment through cartouches: the closed frame
signaled "special content inside — a name," and by comparing cartouches on
the Rosetta Stone he caught the first phonetic signs. The cartouche = the bracket,
and it historically worked as an entry point into decipherment. This is the project
pattern `THE SMART AMATEUR SEES THE GAP`.

## 5. Honest boundaries (project discipline, not overclaim)

- The bracket gets you to `THE SIGN EXISTS + ITS BOUNDARIES + STRUCTURE`, but NOT
  to `THE SIGN MEANS X`. From "this is a word" to "this word means water" you need a
  key or a parallel text (the Rosetta Stone — three scripts).
  The author himself: "the first step toward understanding," not understanding.
- The lesson of the cartouche: the delimiter must visually differ IN APPEARANCE from the
  content — otherwise a descendant will take `(` and `)` for letters. The cartouche
  worked because the oval does not look like the glyphs.
- The trade-off of nesting: the more bracket types, the more the reader
  must first decode the bracket system itself. Richness against
  the load on recognizing the delimiter.
