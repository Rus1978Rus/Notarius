# What we can do now, and what we'll still build

In plain words, no tech. To show the buyer the boundary honestly:
where the product already works, and where the next piece of work lies.

---

## Two different things

There are two different questions about any file. Don't confuse them — that's honesty.

**1. "Is it the same file, or was it touched?"**
A quick check: does the received copy match the original. The answer is coarse —
**"the same / not the same"**, no details.

**2. "Where exactly and what exactly was substituted?"**
A fine check: show the specific place and name the change
("in the third line the amount 1,000,000 became 9,000,000, and the edit was hidden").

---

## What we can do today

| | Text (invoice, act, contract) | Photo / video |
|---|---|---|
| **Same / touched** | ✅ now | ✅ now |
| **Where and what exactly was substituted** | ✅ now | ⛔ needs building |

- **Same / touched — already works for anything.** An invoice, a
  photograph, a video, an archive. Change one byte — we'll see it.
- **Where and what exactly — for now only for text.** Because text has
  lines, words and numbers: they can be compared and named. In a photo it's pixels,
  in a video it's frames; to show "this frame was re-spliced" or "this
  area was forged," you need a separate "reader" for media.

---

## The good news

The model itself is **not tied to text** — it's about any piece of information.
The only thing missing is a "reader" for photo and video (just like the one that already exists
for text). That's realistic to build — it's the next piece of work, not
today's button.

**In short for the buyer:**
right now — we reliably catch substitution in documents and point a finger at where;
for photo and video — we already say "touched or not," while "where exactly" is
on the way.

---

_The honest boundary stays the same: we show where the substitution is — the verdict
on intent is delivered by a human._
