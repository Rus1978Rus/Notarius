# Where it gets embedded: an engine inside other people's programs

In plain words about how the product reaches people. The short takeaway: we are
not a standalone application — we are a **small engine that gets sewn into** the
programs people already use.

---

## Two touches live inside familiar programs

- **Where the document is born** — accounting, billing, CRM, 1C, ERP. Here the
  **mark** is placed (a fingerprint of the real figure) — automatically, on the
  "Send" button.
- **Where the document is received** — the mail client, a messenger, the CRM
  inbox, a portal. Here the **reconciliation** happens on receipt.

The person does nothing extra: sent it as usual — the mark went in; received it as
usual — the reconciliation passed.

---

## Where exactly it can be sewn in

- **mail agents** (Outlook, mail gateways) — a mark on sending, a reconciliation
  on receipt;
- **accounting / billing programs, 1C, ERP** — print every invoice/certificate at
  the moment of issue;
- **CRM** — a mark on an outgoing document, a reconciliation on an incoming one;
- **messengers** — a bot checks a sent file;
- **document-exchange portals, EDI** — the mark and reconciliation right in the
  channel.

Everywhere information is **created** or **transmitted**, either a mark or a
reconciliation is placed.

---

## Ours and not ours

- **Ours** — the engine: take the mark, reconcile, issue the verdict "where and
  what."
- **Not ours** — the "door" itself: the Outlook plugin, the 1C module, the
  messenger bot, the CRM app. Whoever embeds it writes it.

The motor is ours; each person builds the body for their own road. This is the
model: not selling a box, but **licensing an embeddable engine.**

---

## The honest edges (keep in mind, don't hide)

1. **The mark is needed on both sides.** Embedding it only for the recipient
   leaves nothing to reconcile against again. The value appears when the sender
   marks at birth and the recipient reconciles. Adoption is two-sided: strongest
   where both sides are in one ecosystem, or where the mark is placed in a shared
   registry.
2. **The registry "under glass" must be accessible to both.** Inside one company —
   simple. Between companies — you need an agreed shared (or public) registry. This
   is a real adoption question.
3. **The mark must be placed unobtrusively.** People don't press extra buttons. A
   good integration marks by itself, at the moment of "Send."
4. **"Where and what exactly" — for now for text.** For photo/video, right now
   only "touched/not" (see the separate sheet).

---

_In one line: we are an engine inside other people's programs. It places a mark
where the document is born and reconciles where it's received. The doors — mail,
accounting, CRM, messengers — are embedded by the buyer; the motor inside is ours._
