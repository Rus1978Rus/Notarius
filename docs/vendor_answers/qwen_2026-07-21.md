## A) Bloggers, authors, independent media

**1. "Silent substitution" detector in guest publications and syndication**
AUDIENCE: independent authors and investigators. Pain: after handing text to an editor/platform, a link is quietly inserted, a word is changed (a "not" is added), or invisible characters (ZWSP) that break search indexing are slipped in — while authorship is formally preserved.
MECHANISM: the author signs specific paragraphs/quotations with an HMAC envelope before sending; at publication a plugin/web tool compares the fragment against the original hash; on a discrepancy the scanner checks the codepoint length and points to the exact position of the invisible insertion ("in the word «никогда» a U+200B was embedded at position 2").
WHY NOT COVERED: C2PA signs the document at export, but does not track a CMS system changing a specific paragraph after export; in-toto protects the pipeline, not the semantics of the text.
FIRST STEP: a web page (Streamlit/FastAPI): paste text → JSON signature of the fragments; a second mode: article URL → parse → compare → report on invisible characters. 1 week.
SELF-ASSESSMENT: NOVELTY 4, FEASIBILITY 5, DEMAND 4.

**2. "Clean quote" verification for fact-checkers**
AUDIENCE: fact-checkers and independent media. Pain: proving that a quote was published exactly as in the original, without an imperceptible modification (a quote mark swapped for a look-alike Unicode character, an invisible space that breaks search).
MECHANISM: element-level provenance of the quote: source URL, timestamp, hash, a report that no invisible modifiers are present; when someone else publishes "the same" quote — a diff: "visually identical, but a U+200D has been added after the third word."
WHY NOT COVERED: OpenTimestamps does not compare fragments at the codepoint level; C2PA does not work with quotes torn out of someone else's articles.
FIRST STEP: a browser extension/script: select a quote → a short report-link with a hash and scan status. 1–2 weeks.
SELF-ASSESSMENT: NOVELTY 3, FEASIBILITY 4, DEMAND 3.

## B) People working with AI

**3. RAG Context Poisoning Auditor**
AUDIENCE: RAG developers and AI-agent operators. Pain: an attacker plants in the knowledge base a document visually identical to a legitimate one, but with invisible characters (a ZWJ between letters) — evading vector-search deduplication or breaking the tokenizer, leading to a malicious answer.
MECHANISM: at indexing, each chunk gets element-level provenance: hash + a report on invisible characters; at query time a retrieved chunk that visually matches the reference but has a different codepoint size → a warning "codepoint anomaly in chunk #42, possible context poisoning."
WHY NOT COVERED: SLSA/in-toto protect the build and deployment of the RAG application, but do not verify the integrity of individual fragments from the vector DB at query time.
FIRST STEP: a Python script: a folder of texts → a JSON report per chunk (hash + invisible-character flag); a demo on two visually identical files, one with a hidden injection. 1 week.
SELF-ASSESSMENT: NOVELTY 5, FEASIBILITY 4, DEMAND 4.

**4. "AI vs. Human" editing tracker with detector-evasion detection**
AUDIENCE: teachers, editors of AI text. Pain: students/authors generate text with AI and insert invisible characters (U+200B, U+2060) to break AI-generation detectors and plagiarism checks.
MECHANISM: the AI tool signs paragraphs as AI_NATIVE; the user's edits are signed as HUMAN_EDITED; the report shows fragment authorship + a flag: "in paragraph 2 (HUMAN_EDITED) 3 invisible U+200B characters were found, characteristic of detector evasion."
WHY NOT COVERED: C2PA can mark a document as AI-created, but does not track character-by-character editing and does not specialize in detecting invisible characters used to evade checks.
FIRST STEP: a web interface: two texts (original AI + final) → per-paragraph diff, signature status (emulated), highlighting of invisible characters with a warning. 2 weeks.
SELF-ASSESSMENT: NOVELTY 4, FEASIBILITY 4, DEMAND 5.

## C) Other area (legal/administrative)

**5. "Term substitution" detector in standard contracts for freelancers**
AUDIENCE: freelancers, small business. Pain: the client sends a "standard" contract, visually the same, but in a key clause a figure has been changed or an invisible character added — so the clause cannot be found via Ctrl+F during a dispute, or so it breaks parsing by a legal AI.
MECHANISM: the user uploads a signed reference template (clauses as elements), then pastes in the received contract; comparison by element: "Clause 4.2 changed. Visual text matches, but a U+FEFF was found, breaking machine processing."
WHY NOT COVERED: BEC anti-fraud reconciles payment details against a database, not the integrity of text at the level of invisible characters; legal diff systems show visible changes but ignore/mis-render invisible Unicode characters.
FIRST STEP: a script: two files (reference + received) → a per-paragraph table: hash status, codepoint-length difference, invisible characters found. 1 week.
SELF-ASSESSMENT: NOVELTY 4, FEASIBILITY 5, DEMAND 3.

## Vendor self-rejection

Rejects #2 "Clean quote verification": collapses on FEASIBILITY and DEMAND due to the "cold start" and the asymmetry of effort — the source of the quote must sign it BEFORE handing it to the journalist, and sources have neither the motivation nor the skills; the journalist cannot unilaterally create evidentiary provenance for someone else's quote (there is no native state to compare against). Resources are better directed at ideas where the user controls both the original and the final document (#1, #4, #5).
