## A) Bloggers, authors, independent media

**1. "Direct speech under the microscope" — proving a quotation is genuine**
AUDIENCE: an investigative journalist who has published an interview; readers/editors/the court doubt whether the direct quotes were invented.
MECHANISM: a signed "interview transcript" block (segments with text and timestamps); each quotation in the article is an element referencing a transcript segment, its hash, and its position. Chain: phrase in the article ← transcript fragment ← audio recording (external timestamp). A hand-assembled quotation → an explicit break in the report.
WHY NOT COVERED: C2PA confirms that audio was created by a device, but does not track the turning of an audio fragment into a text quotation; timestamping does not link a paragraph to its source.
FIRST STEP: JSON transcript with HMAC → article with quotation fields (hash + offset of the parent block) → verifier with an HTML report "native/inserted." 1–2 weeks.
SELF-ASSESSMENT: NOVELTY 4, FEASIBILITY 5, DEMAND 4.

**2. "Guest post without forgery" — protection against hidden edits after publication**
AUDIENCE: a blogger who runs guest articles; the "guest vs. editor" dispute over inserted links/edits.
MECHANISM: the post as an array of signed elements (paragraphs, headings, links); publication stores the author's hash of each element + the publisher's signature; any word change breaks the element's lineage; invisible insertions are caught by the built-in Unicode scanner.
WHY NOT COVERED: C2PA signs the container without distinguishing the author's parts from the editor's; Git requires a shared repository and gives no proof to an external reader.
FIRST STEP: extend the envelope — two-layer signatures (author + editor), a table "element — author's hash — matches?". 2 weeks.
SELF-ASSESSMENT: NOVELTY 3, FEASIBILITY 5, DEMAND 3.

## B) People working with AI

**3. "Birth certificate for a fact" — provenance of RAG-system answers**
AUDIENCE: a RAG developer/auditor; the regulator needs proof that the answer's facts come from a verified base rather than a hallucination, and which record produced each statement.
MECHANISM: each chunk of the base is a signed block; each statement of the answer (a span) is bound to its source blocks; a provenance graph "sentence → chunk → document → author" with signatures on the nodes; breaks are visible to a human.
WHY NOT COVERED: LangSmith/Arize show the chunks in the prompt, but not a cryptographically verifiable trace at the level of the answer's elements with the provenance of the chunks themselves; C2PA is media-focused; in-toto is about the build, not inference.
FIRST STEP: 10 signed chunks + a local LLM + post-processing (sentence overlap with chunks) → a signed answer envelope with anchors. 2 weeks.
SELF-ASSESSMENT: NOVELTY 4, FEASIBILITY 4, DEMAND 5.

**4. "Prompt detective" — a prompt's lineage and detecting attacks via invisible characters**
AUDIENCE: a prompt engineer with complex instruction chains; a teacher checking students' prompts for plagiarism and hidden injections.
MECHANISM: the provenance of each element of the prompt (lines, instructions, examples); a paste from an external source → flagged "non-native," the break is recorded; the Unicode scanner shows at which editing stage the invisible characters appeared and from which source.
WHY NOT COVERED: prompt-injection scanners (rebuff, lakera) detect anomalies, but do not build a verifiable provenance tree of fragments; C2PA signs the prompt as a file without seeing the splice with the invisible attack.
FIRST STEP: a prototype notepad with an operations log (each save is a block with lines and hashes), highlighting of non-native lines on paste from the clipboard + the built-in Unicode scanner. 2 weeks.
SELF-ASSESSMENT: NOVELTY 5, FEASIBILITY 4, DEMAND 5.

## Other area

**5. "Review auditor" — proving a consumer review is original**
AUDIENCE: a marketplace user or a regulator investigating fake reviews; a seller copies a positive review with bots.
MECHANISM: the review is split into sentences; each is signed with a key tied to a confirmed purchase; a copied review → many leaves with one original hash but foreign signatures; visualization "first appeared at X; for the rest — a break."
WHY NOT COVERED: platforms use behavioral analysis/IP, but not cryptographic proof that a text element is original; OpenTimestamps does not prove primacy among identical copies.
FIRST STEP: a review server + sentence signing with Ed25519 using a dummy purchase token + a graph utility highlighting plagiarism. 1–2 weeks.
SELF-ASSESSMENT: NOVELTY 4, FEASIBILITY 3, DEMAND 4.

## Vendor self-rejection

Rejects #2 "Guest post without forgery": least novel and least in demand; the "author–editor" conflict is settled by both sides signing the final PDF (C2PA covers this for whole documents); word-by-word provenance is overkill for most blogs; demand is narrow, and non-cryptographic practices already exist.
