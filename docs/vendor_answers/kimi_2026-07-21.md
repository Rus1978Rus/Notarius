## Area A — bloggers, authors, independent media

**1. "Acceptance"**
AUDIENCE: an editor at a content studio / owner of a niche site who buys texts from freelancers. Pain: pays for original writing, gets a rehash of someone else's work or untouched AI output; plagiarism checkers catch only verbatim matches.
MECHANISM: the freelancer signs paragraphs at checkpoints (outline → draft → final) with the prototype envelope; at acceptance the editor checks which fragments have a history going back to the draft and which appeared "out of nowhere" in the final; the invisible-insertion scanner reports traces of importing from outside. Report: "the last third of the article has no history — it appeared all at once an hour before delivery." Candidly: this works only as a contractual process — without the author's cooperation it is just another plagiarism checker again.
WHY NOT COVERED: Copyscape/Grammarly give a "looks like the web" signal, not a provenance chain; C2PA covers whole assets inside the pipeline, not fragments across the "freelancer ↔ client" boundary; timestamping is existence, not lineage. A July 2026 search finds no category of "provenance of a text fragment."
FIRST STEP: a CLI built on the ready-made envelope — signing paragraphs at three points + verify with a "fragments without history" report. Demo on one genuinely purchased text, one week.
SELF-ASSESSMENT: NOVELTY 4 · FEASIBILITY 4 · DEMAND 3 (sales channel via studios/platforms).

**2. "My own trace"**
AUDIENCE: a blogger/small publisher whose material is reprinted by aggregators without attribution. Pain: primacy is provable by a timestamp, but "copied specifically from my page, not from a common source" is not provable.
MECHANISM: at publication the fragments are signed and invisible per-fragment marks are woven into the text (the project can already scan for them); a scan of someone else's page: "fragments 2, 5, 6 carry my marks dated <date> — a copy from my page." Candid about the limit: the marks survive copy-paste, die on reprinting/normalization/CMS cleanup — a one-directional signal: presence proves, absence does not refute.
WHY NOT COVERED: industry watermarks are about media (SynthID, Digimarc — pixels; C2PA metadata is stripped by a screenshot); canary traps are for leaks inside organizations, not for a public author. The closest analogue is canary tokens; for bloggers it is not productized.
FIRST STEP: a script "weave marks → publish" + "scan someone else's page → report." Demo: my own blog vs. a copy-paste clone, 1 week.
SELF-ASSESSMENT: NOVELTY 3 · FEASIBILITY 4 · DEMAND 2 (the path to enforcement is unclear, the marks are fragile).

## Area B — people working with AI

**3. "The human layer"**
AUDIENCE: an editor of AI text at a publication/content department; secondarily a teacher. Pain: the publisher/regulator demands proof of which fragments a human wrote. AI detectors guess and err in both directions. Regulatory hook: EU AI Act, Recital 134 — the "AI-generated" label can be lifted only with "meaningful human review" backed by an audit-ready trace.
MECHANISM: at the moment of pasting from an AI tool the fragment is wrapped in markers and signed (a paste-hook); every human edit is a new link with a signature. Report: "untouched AI — 42%, AI + edit — 38%, human from scratch — 20%." This is provenance, not a classifier: it does not guess, it shows a self-declared chain with tamper control; anything that bypasses the hook is a candid "provenance not declared."
WHY NOT COVERED: detectors are probabilistic classifiers with no chain; C2PA Actions Assertions log edits inside the media pipeline and die on copy-paste of text between tools; "humanizers" work in the opposite direction.
FIRST STEP: a hotkey script "paste from clipboard with marker + signature" + a scanner report. Demo: one mixed text, two weeks.
SELF-ASSESSMENT: NOVELTY 4 · FEASIBILITY 3 · DEMAND 4 (regulation pulls demand; the friction of the hook cuts feasibility).

**4. "Grounding the answer" (RAG)**
AUDIENCE: a RAG developer on a small team (legal/medical reference). Pain: a citation to a chunk may be fabricated or distorted.
MECHANISM: chunks are signed at indexing; the answer's fragments are forced to reference chunk hashes; verify: was the chunk in the retrieval? does the content match? Report: "fragment 3 references a chunk that was not in the context."
WHY NOT COVERED — candidly: ALMOST COVERED. Claim-level provenance is an industry standard-direction; verification frameworks already check statements against chunks (RAGAS/TruLens; CMU+Anthropic: −73% errors); tracing — LangSmith/Langfuse. The open gap is narrow — portable proof for an external verifier — but demand from a small user is questionable.
FIRST STEP: a toy RAG over 10 documents; verify catches a planted fake citation. One week.
SELF-ASSESSMENT: NOVELTY 2 · FEASIBILITY 2 · DEMAND 3.

**5. "Agent log"**
AUDIENCE: an independent consultant / small studio running AI agents for clients (research, reports). Pain: the client asks "where did the agent get this?" — you need a verifiable chain "output ← tool ← data" outside the agent platform.
MECHANISM: each tool output (search, DB, file) is signed on receipt; the final report is marked up by fragments with references to the input hashes; verify: which statements are backed by signed inputs, which are "from the model's head." The client gets a human-readable report of the breaks.
WHY NOT COVERED: the enterprise field is dense — why-trails (MightyBot), evidence packs with append-only storage (KLA), cryptographic agent identities, OpenTelemetry, Collibra — but all of it is SIEM/GRC stacks inside the perimeter of regulated industries. For a small user outside closed networks there is no portable "prove it to the client" — word for word the project's stated niche.
FIRST STEP: a mock agent with two tools: sign the inputs, mark up the report, verify + report to the client. Two weeks.
SELF-ASSESSMENT: NOVELTY 3 · FEASIBILITY 3 · DEMAND 3 (the candid bet — get ahead of the enterprise stacks at the bottom of the market).

## Vendor self-rejection

Rejects #4 "Grounding the answer": (1) the densest prior art of all five — claim-level citation is documented as a standard-direction, and verification frameworks already check against chunks; (2) the worst feasibility — fragment↔chunk alignment requires an ML stack, so the solo variant is either a toy or a worse copy; (3) strategically redundant: the "portable proof" gap is closed more strongly by the "Agent log" (#5), where RAG is a special case.

## Vendor's overarching caveat

Four of the five ideas depend on cooperation at the boundary (the contractor signs, the user sets the hook, the developer instruments) — Notarius proves the chain only where someone started keeping it. This is the main demand filter; the shortest road belongs to the two that build on the existing prototype: "Acceptance" and "My own trace."
